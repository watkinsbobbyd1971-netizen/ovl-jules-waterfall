The '''Charlottetown meteorite''' was a [[meteorite fall]] observed on July 25, 2024. It is notable as the first meteorite known with video and audio of the impact recorded and as the only known meteorite fall in [[Prince Edward Island]], Canada.<ref name=Brun2025>{{cite news |first=Stephen |last=Brun |work=CBC News |title=P.E.I. homeowner captures sound and video of meteorite strike on camera, and scientists believe it's a first |date=January 13, 2025 |url=https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/prince-edward-island/pei-charlottetown-meteorite-strike-first-audio-1.7430018 }}</ref>

The Charlottetown meteorite is classified as [[H chondrite|H5 ordinary chondrite]].<ref name=MeteoriticalBulletin>{{cite web |url=https://www.lpi.usra.edu/meteor/metbull.php?code=84378 |website=Meteoritical Bulletin Database |title=Charlottetown }}</ref> Its impact left a divot measuring about {{cvt|2|cm|in}} across.

== Impact ==

After an evening walk in the Marshfield neighbourhood of [[Charlottetown, Prince Edward Island]], Laura Kelly and her partner Joe Velaidum found a starburst pattern of gray dust on their sidewalk. They cleared it away, not knowing what it was. Upon discovering home [[security camera]] footage of the impact, they suspected it was a meteorite.<ref name=MeteoriticalBulletin /> They contacted researchers, connecting with Chris Herd of the [[University of Alberta]] Meteorite Collection, and collected material from the impact.<ref>{{cite web |title=What does a space rock sound like when it hits the ground? First ever meteorite to land in Prince Edward Island is caught on camera, makes auditory history and ends up in U of A collection |url=https://www.ualberta.ca/en/news/news-releases-and-statements/news-releases/2025/jan/what-does-a-space-rock-sound-like-when-it-hits-the-ground-first-ever-meteorite-to-land-in-prince-edward-island-is-caught-on-camera-makes-auditory-history-and-ends-up-in-u-of-a-collection.html |date=January 13, 2025 |website=University of Alberta |access-date=January 15, 2025 |archive-date=January 15, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250115085444/https://www.ualberta.ca/en/news/news-releases-and-statements/news-releases/2025/jan/what-does-a-space-rock-sound-like-when-it-hits-the-ground-first-ever-meteorite-to-land-in-prince-edward-island-is-caught-on-camera-makes-auditory-history-and-ends-up-in-u-of-a-collection.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

The recorded sound of the impact has been described as similar to breaking ice.<ref name=Brun2025 /> The impact created a divot some {{convert|2|cm|in}} in diameter.<ref name=MeteoriticalBulletin /> Velaidum reports that he was standing at that location minutes before the meteorite struck it; he considers the event "a [[near-death experience]]".<ref name=Bassi2025>{{cite magazine |first=Margherita |last=Bassi |date=January 16, 2025 |magazine=Smithsonian Magazine |title=Meteorite Crash in Canada Is Caught by Home Security Camera in Likely World-First Video Footage |url=https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/meteorite-crash-in-canada-is-caught-by-home-security-camera-in-likely-world-first-video-footage-180985847/ |access-date=January 18, 2025 |archive-date=January 19, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250119073219/https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/meteorite-crash-in-canada-is-caught-by-home-security-camera-in-likely-world-first-video-footage-180985847/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

The meteorite came from the [[asteroid belt]], according to Herd, and would have traveled at least {{convert|200|km/h|mph|0}} just prior to impact.{{r|Bassi2025|Brun2025}} According to Bob King, he estimates a diameter of {{convert|6|-|7|cm|in}} for the impactor.<ref>{{Cite web |last=King |first=Bob |date=2025-01-22 |title=Witness the First-ever Sight and Sound Recording of a Meteorite Impact |url=https://skyandtelescope.org/astronomy-news/hear-the-first-ever-recording-of-a-meteorite-slamming-into-the-ground/ |access-date=2026-10-23 |website=Sky & Telescope |language=en-US}}</ref>

== Composition ==

Recovered fragments of the meteorite, ranging from less than {{convert|1|mm|spell=in}} up to {{convert|7|mm|spell=in}} across, are made up of (per Herd) "very fine grained, mostly translucent crystals". It also contains larger, translucent white and gray crystals and metallic fragments.<ref name=MeteoriticalBulletin />
