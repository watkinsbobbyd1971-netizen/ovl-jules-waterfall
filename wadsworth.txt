'''Wadsworth''' is a rare [[HED meteorite|HED]] meteorite that fell in the US state of [[Ohio]] on 17 March 2026. A bright daytime [[bolide]] was seen streaking through the sky over [[Cleveland]] before fragmenting.<ref name="sciam">{{Cite web |last=Mogensen |first=Jackie Flynn |title=An asteroid just exploded above Ohio with the force of 250 tons of TNT |url=https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/an-asteroid-just-exploded-above-ohio-with-the-force-of-250-tons-of-tnt/ |access-date=2026-05-01 |website=Scientific American |language=en}}</ref><ref name="signal cleveland">{{Cite web |last1=Wild |first1=Franziska |last2=Indriolo |first2=Michael |last3=Castele |first3=Nick |date=2026-03-17 |title=Sonic boom? Experts break down Northeast Ohio's rare event |url=https://signalcleveland.org/meteor-sonic-boom-near-cleveland-northeast-ohio-march-2026/ |access-date=2026-05-01 |website=Signal Cleveland |language=en-US}}</ref><ref name="fireballs">{{Cite web |title=Event 20260317-125642 |url=https://fireballs.ndc.nasa.gov/skyfalls/events/20260317-125642 |access-date=2026-05-01 |website=NASA's All Sky Fireball Network}}</ref> With an estimated [[Air burst|airburst]] energy equal to {{Convert|250|tTNT|J}}, it created a [[sonic boom]] heard dozens of miles from the event.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Dolce |first=Chris |date=2026-03-17 |title=Rare fireball spotted over eastern US caused a sonic boom |url=https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/17/weather/fireball-meteor-boom-ohio-cleveland-climate |access-date=2026-05-01 |website=CNN |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Brackett |first=Ron |date=2026-03-18 |title=NASA Confirms Loud Boom Triggered By Asteroid Seen Over At Least 10 States |url=https://weather.com/science/space/news/2026-03-17-cleveland-boom-meteor-national-weather-service |access-date=2026-05-01 |website=The Weather Channel |language=en-US}}</ref><ref name="sciam" /><ref name="signal cleveland"/><ref name="space.com">{{Cite web |first=Anthony |last=Wood |date=2026-03-19 |title=Ohio fireball may have dropped meteorites between Akron and Canton — should you pick it up if you find one? |url=https://www.space.com/stargazing/meteor-showers/explosive-daytime-fireball-may-have-dropped-meteorites-on-ohio-heres-where-to-look-and-what-to-do-if-you-find-one |access-date=2026-05-03 |website=Space |language=en}}</ref>

The fireball was captured by the [[Geostationary Lightning Mapper|GLM]] instrument aboard geostationary satellite [[GOES-19]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=2026-03-17 |title=GOES-19 GLM captures the signature of a bolide over Lake Erie and northern Ohio |url=https://cimss.ssec.wisc.edu/satellite-blog/archives/69475 |access-date=2026-05-01 |website=[[CIMSS]] |language=en-US}}</ref>

== Meteorites ==
The first pieces were found by meteorite hunters from [[Connecticut]], Roberto Vargas and Matt Perison. These stones were 12.2 and 1.75 grams in mass.<ref name="karmaka">{{Cite web |title=WADSWORTH meteorite fall (>1728 g, Eucrite) near Wadsworth (Medina County) and Rittman (Wayne County), Ohio, USA at 12:56:42 UTC on 17 March 2026 |url=https://karmaka.de/?p=40548 |access-date=2026-05-03 |website=Karmaka Meteorites}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |first=Emilee |last=Speck |date=2026-03-19 |title=Ohio man finds possible meteorite after bright fireball streaks across 12 states |url=https://www.accuweather.com/en/space-news/ohio-man-finds-possible-meteorite-after-bright-fireball-streaks-across-12-states/1874770 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260320083548/https://www.accuweather.com/en/space-news/ohio-man-finds-possible-meteorite-after-bright-fireball-streaks-across-12-states/1874770 |archive-date=2026-03-20 |access-date=2026-05-03 |language=en-us |publisher=[[AccuWeather]] |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last1=Thyret |first1=Julia |last2=Williams |first2=Avery |last3=Molesky |first3=Colton |last4=McCarthy |first4=Caitlin |last5=Grant |first5=Keith |last6=Vadaj |first6=Rachel |date=2026-03-19 |title=Meteorite hunters flock to Northeast Ohio after fireball breaks apart over Medina County |url=https://www.cleveland19.com/2026/03/19/meteorite-hunters-states-away-find-fragments-northeast-ohio/ |access-date=2026-05-03 |website=[[Cleveland 19]] |language=en}}</ref> Soon after, many more fragments were found; so far the largest known fragment from this fall, which was found by Mark Sokol and others on 20 March 2026, weighs {{Convert|509.9|g|lboz|0}}.<ref name="karmaka" /> A total of {{Convert|1,728|g|lboz|0}} were found.<ref name="lpi" /><ref name="karmaka" /> This meteorite was scientifically classified by the Ohio Meteorite Classification (OMC) Team as a monomict eucrite.<ref name="lpi" />
