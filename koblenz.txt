At 18:55 [[Central European Time|CET]] on 8 March 2026, a bright [[Meteor#Fireball|fireball]] was seen across Western Europe traveling from the southwest.<ref name="IMO20261467"/>

==History==
The fireball, caused by a [[meteor]] estimated to be up to a few meters in diameter,<ref name="ESA20260309"/> disintegrated in an [[Meteor air burst|air burst]] at an altitude of about {{convert|50|km|mi}} and scattered [[meteorite]] fragments across western Germany.<ref name="FR20260309"/> Some falling fragments damaged residential buildings in [[Koblenz]] and the [[Eifel]] and [[Hunsrück]] areas. One fragment smashed a football-sized hole through the roof of a residential building in the district of Güls in Koblenz, damaging a bedroom. Though people were in the building at that time, no injuries were reported.<ref name="FR20260309"/><ref name="Merkurist20260308"/> Several centimeter-sized fragments were quickly recovered from the damaged building.<ref name="Focus20260308"/> The recovered fragments were identified as probable [[chondrite]]s, the most common type of stony meteorite, though definitive identification requires laboratory analysis.<ref name="FR20260309"/>

{{asof|2026|3|20}}, 3,228 sightings of the fireball were reported to the [[International Meteor Organization]].<ref name="IMO20261467"/> Emergency phone calls in Koblenz surged following the fireball's passage;<ref name="FR20260309"/> local police quickly ruled out a rocket strike or plane crash.<ref name="RTL20260309"/><ref name="Tagesschau20260309"/>
